Why It Matters

Real Changes. Parents Are Already Seeing Them.

Backed by decades of research across 65 studies worldwide — these aren't feel-good promises. They are measurable outcomes that show up at school, at home, and in your child's future.

Grades Actually Improve

A meta-analysis of 65 independent studies found that children who learn to code score 16 percentile points higher on cognitive ability tests. Chess students in Texas showed 2× the improvement in maths and reading (Grades 3–5) compared to peers who didn't play. This isn't motivation talk — it's measurable.

Concentration That Sticks

After just 8 coding lessons, children showed measurable improvements in focus, planning, and impulse control (Frontiers in Psychology). Chess demands sustained strategic thinking — proven in clinical trials to improve attention even in children diagnosed with ADHD. The child who couldn't sit for 20 minutes will sit for 90.

Maths Stops Being a Problem

Mathematics is Kenya's most failed KCSE subject — year after year, students score D+ because they memorise instead of understand. Coding makes abstract maths visual and verifiable: coordinates, sequences, logic, patterns. When a child builds a game, they're doing algebra without knowing it. Then the test comes — and they know it.

Critical Thinking — Not Just Common Sense

A 5-year Pennsylvania study found chess students improved critical thinking by 17.3% per year — compared to just 4.56% for peers in other enrichment activities. In Venezuela, 4,000+ second-graders showed such significant IQ gains after 4.5 months of chess that the government added it to the national curriculum.

Screen Time Becomes an Asset

65.4% of Nairobi preschoolers already exceed WHO screen time guidelines (University of Nairobi). But research draws a sharp line: passive screens (TikTok, YouTube) reduce attention and school readiness by up to 52%. Active coding builds it. We don't ask your child to put the screen down — we change what they do with it.

Confidence That Transfers Everywhere

The moment a child finishes their first working game, something shifts permanently. Research shows confident children are 20% more likely to pursue STEM subjects and careers. Chess teaches children to lose with dignity, analyse their mistakes, and come back stronger. That is a life skill no classroom test can teach.

"My son used to avoid any question he didn't immediately know the answer to. Three months in, his teacher called me — she said he's now the first to put his hand up and try. Whatever you're doing, keep doing it."

— Parent, Nairobi (Term-Time Student)
