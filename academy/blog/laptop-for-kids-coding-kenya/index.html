Buying a laptop for a child learning to code in Kenya: what to check before you pay

December is laptop season in many Kenyan homes. Before you pay, look past the price tag at four things: the processor, the version of Windows, the battery and the warranty.

Walk down Luthuli Avenue in December and you’ll see the same scene in shop after shop. A parent, a child, and two laptops open on the counter. The attendant taps one of them: “This one is Core i7.” The parent looks at the price, then at the child, and reaches for the wallet.

A child learning to code needs less machine than most parents expect. The money goes wrong somewhere else: in an old version of Windows, a tired battery and a warranty nobody wrote down.

“A more expensive laptop helps my child learn faster”

Look at what a beginner runs. Scratch works in a web browser. A first Python program, a quiz or a guessing game, runs on almost any laptop sold in the last ten years. A Zoom class needs a webcam, a microphone and a steady connection.

None of this needs a gaming laptop or the newest model. A refurbished business laptop with 8GB of memory and an SSD handles all of it with room to spare. Business models such as the HP EliteBook and Dell Latitude were built for office use, with sturdy keyboards, and they fill most of the refurbished listings we checked.

So the spec decision is quick. The harder questions come after you’ve picked a model.

Same laptop, three prices

We looked up one model, the HP EliteBook 840 G5 with a Core i5 8th generation chip, 8GB of memory and a 256GB SSD, at three Kenyan shops on the same day.

The cheapest and the dearest are about KSh 14,000 apart for the same model on paper. Part of the gap is the warranty: six months at one shop, a year at another, nothing stated at the third. Lapsol’s price also leaves out VAT, so it looks cheaper online than it is at the till.

The price tells you about the shop. The receipt tells you about the laptop.

Ask what the extra money pays for. If the answer is a longer warranty in writing, it’s buying you something. If the answer is “it’s a better one”, ask the attendant to show you how.

The Windows 10 problem

Microsoft ended support for Windows 10 on 14 October 2025. Home users had the option to pay for a year of extra security updates, and those ended on 13 October 2026. Today, a laptop running Windows 10 gets no security fixes at all.

Many refurbished laptops in Kenyan shops still ship with it. One of the three listings above names Windows 10 Pro in its title. A child’s laptop goes online every day, for classes, homework and games. It should be getting updates.

Microsoft’s list of processors for Windows 11 includes Intel’s 8th generation and newer. Read the generation from the processor name on the sticker or in the shop’s listing:

  • Core i5-8350U: the first digit after the dash is 8, so 8th generation
  • Core i5-7200U: 7th generation, below the list
  • Core i5-10210U: the first two digits are 10, so 10th generation

Ask the shop to start the laptop and open Settings, then System, then About. Check it says Windows 11 and shows as activated.

The battery test, in the shop

A refurbished laptop’s battery has already lived one life. Windows records how much it has lost, and you’ll read it in two minutes.

Open PowerShell or Command Prompt and type powercfg /batteryreport. Windows saves a file called battery-report.html in the user folder. Open it and compare two numbers: the design capacity, what the battery held when new, and the full charge capacity, what it holds today.

How-To Geek’s example shows a battery designed for 44,400 mWh holding 37,685 mWh, about 85% of its original charge. Decide your own floor before you go. A battery at half its design capacity means a laptop tied to the socket, and in a home with power cuts, a lost class.

Take a pen and a USB mouse to the shop. Type every key in Notepad, plug in the charger, run the battery report and write the laptop’s serial number on the receipt next to the warranty period.

Ask what the laptop is for

Most parents skip one question. A year from now, what will your child have done on this laptop?

If the honest answer is YouTube and games, the spec won’t matter, and the laptop becomes a bigger phone. If the answer is “built three games and learnt to type”, the laptop needs a place to live in the house, a time when it’s used, and a reason to open it. Check the prices yourself

Prices move every week, and our numbers are from one day in November. Open three listings for the same model, compare the warranty on each, and ask every shop the same questions: which Windows, which processor generation, which battery health, how long a warranty, and is it on the receipt.
