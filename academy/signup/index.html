The three questions parents ask me first

“Isn’t this more screen time?”

Your child will spend time on screens whatever you plan. What changes their week is whether they watch or build. In class, they build. The rest of the day stays yours to manage, and I won’t ask you to take the phone away.

“AI writes code now. Is coding still worth learning?”

More than before. AI produces code fast, and it’s often wrong in ways a beginner won’t spot. Children who have built programs themselves notice the mistakes, ask sharper questions and decide what gets built. Those are the skills we teach.

“Is this a game club with a certificate?”

No. Your child finishes a project, records chess games in notation and gets written feedback from the teacher. If all they did was have fun, I’d count your money as wasted, and I’d rather say so now.

What the lessons cover

Coding: Scratch first, then Python

Younger children start by snapping code blocks together in Scratch, inside the browser. Within a few weeks they’re running a game of their own design, with rules they wrote. Older children who are ready to type move on to Python.

  • Sequences, variables, conditions and coordinates
  • Debugging: guess why it broke, test the guess, fix it
  • One finished project, theirs to keep and share
  • Python for older children once they’re ready

Chess: notation, tactics and reviewing every loss

Children learn to write their games down and go back over what went wrong. Nobody gets left alone to push pieces around.

  • Algebraic notation, to record and replay their own games
  • Openings with the reasons behind them, not memorised lines
  • Tactics like forks and pins, built up by repetition
  • Tournaments inside the group, with a review after each game

A puzzle from the first week

The white knight on c7 attacks the king and the rook at the same time. This pattern is called a fork. Black has to save the king, and the rook falls. Children spot this pattern in their own games within days, then start setting it up on purpose.

Will chess raise exam marks?

You’ll see programmes promise it. The strongest reviews of the research don’t support it: chess makes children better at chess, and the effect on grades is small. I won’t claim it.

What I stand behind is narrower. Longer focus, the habit of reviewing a lost game, and a child who reads a game back to you in notation.

Questions from parents

Do you offer a free trial class?

No. Classes run like school: you apply, I place your child in the right group, and they start. If you want to see how it goes before paying for a month, ask me on WhatsApp about paying week by week.

Is six too young?

Six is where most groups start. At six it’s mostly Scratch, plus chess through small games, paced for short attention spans.

Does my child need any experience?

None. Many children join having never coded or played a full game of chess. Others already play. A few questions before your child starts sort out which group fits.

Will my child keep up?

Groups are set by ability, not age alone. A fast seven-year-old and a careful twelve-year-old sit in different groups, so nobody starts somewhere they’re lost.

What do we need at home, and how much data does it use?

A laptop, tablet or desktop with a camera, plus a steady connection. Nothing to install, Scratch runs in the browser. Headphones help. With video on at Zoom’s standard quality, a class uses up to about 700 MB an hour: around 1 GB for a term class and 2 GB for a holiday morning. Home Wi-Fi works out cheaper than a phone bundle.

Is it safe? Do you record the classes?

Groups are capped at 15 and led by one teacher you know by name. Children join from home with a parent present at the start. Sessions aren’t recorded, nothing gets published, and children don’t message each other.

My child is shy. Will they cope?

Shy children often take to chess fastest, because the board doesn’t ask them to speak up in front of everyone. Coding works the same way. The feedback comes from the program, not from someone judging them.

Is this holiday tuition?

No. We don’t teach the school syllabus or exam work. These are coding and chess classes, run by a private academy, live online.

Will it help at school?

Indirectly, and I won’t overstate it. Kenya approved a coding curriculum for schools in 2022, so these skills line up with where school is heading. The work also builds patience with hard problems and the habit of checking your own reasoning, which maths rewards.

What if my child loses interest?

Usually the group level is wrong. Tell me in the first two or three weeks and I’ll move them to a group at the right level.

We’re not in Nairobi.

Classes run live on Zoom, so children join from anywhere in Kenya. Where you live only affects which timetable suits you.
